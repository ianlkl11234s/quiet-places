Transform the provided reference image or concept into a scene from
“Quiet Places / 靜隅”.

Do not simply apply a visual filter.
Reinterpret the original space according to the following visual language.

CORE CONCEPT
A quiet corner of everyday life where almost nothing happens,
but light, air, weather, and one living presence make the space feel alive.

The scene should feel physically believable first,
and subtly surreal only after looking at it for a while.

────────────────────────

1. PRESERVE THE ORIGINAL SPACE

Preserve the essential architectural identity, spatial proportions,
camera direction, and recognizable structure of the reference.

Simplify rather than redesign.

Remove unnecessary furniture, decorations, signage, clutter,
ornamental architecture, and distracting objects.

Do not turn the location into a museum,
luxury house, fantasy environment, aquarium,
sci-fi installation, or theatrical stage.

The space should remain ordinary, modest, quiet, and believable.

────────────────────────

2. EMPTY SPACE

Negative space is essential.

Approximately 60–80% of the composition may remain visually quiet.

Avoid filling empty areas with additional objects.

Emptiness itself must be part of the composition.

The viewer should feel that this place existed before they arrived
and will continue existing after they leave.

────────────────────────

3. ONE MAIN SUBJECT

Introduce or preserve only ONE clear visual protagonist.

Possible protagonists include:
a plant,
a small school of fish,
a ray,
a jellyfish,
a tree branch,
a suspended water surface,
a simple everyday object,
or the user-provided subject.

The protagonist should occupy only a small part of the frame.

Do not place it prominently like a product display.

Prefer positioning it near the boundary between light and shadow.

It may occasionally disappear partially into darkness.

────────────────────────

4. ONE IMPOSSIBLE THING

Allow only one subtly impossible or dreamlike phenomenon.

For example:
fish swimming above a dry floor,
a ray slowly passing through an empty room,
jellyfish drifting beside a rainy wall,
a piece of ocean visible inside architecture,
or another user-specified surreal element.

Everything else must remain physically convincing.

Do not add glowing magical particles,
fantasy portals,
floating crystals,
neon lights,
or unrelated surreal objects.

The surrealism should feel discovered rather than announced.

────────────────────────

5. LIGHT IS THE PRIMARY COMPOSITIONAL ELEMENT

Use one dominant natural light source.

Possible sources:
a skylight,
a narrow window,
an opening in a wall,
sunlight reflected from water,
overcast daylight,
late-afternoon sun,
moonlight,
or light entering from outside the frame.

The direction of light must be immediately readable.

Most of the room should remain genuinely dark.

Do not use broad ambient illumination to reveal everything.

Light should reveal only selected surfaces.

Strong contrast between illuminated and shadowed regions.

Highlights may be bright,
but shadows should retain depth.

Avoid HDR-like lifted blacks.

────────────────────────

6. LIGHT MUST INTERACT WITH THE ENVIRONMENT

Light should create visible physical consequences:

soft contact shadows,
long directional shadows,
leaf shadows,
water caustics,
reflected light,
light scattering in mist,
subtle volumetric shafts,
or moving illumination caused by water or foliage.

Volumetric light must remain subtle.

It should feel like the air becomes visible,
not like a theatrical spotlight.

────────────────────────

7. NATURAL MOTION

Every Quiet Places scene should contain at least one slow,
continuous natural motion.

Possible motion systems:

wind moving leaves,
occasional gusts,
rainfall,
water ripples,
moving caustics,
drifting fog,
floating dust,
slow clouds,
plant movement,
or gentle animal movement.

Most motion should be almost imperceptible.

Avoid constant repetitive oscillation.

Use irregular timing.

The environment should remain nearly still most of the time,
with occasional small events.

Example wind behavior:

80–90% of the time:
almost no visible wind,
only tiny leaf tremors.

Occasionally:
a soft gust passes through,
branches bend slightly,
leaves respond at different delays,
then gradually return to rest.

Never animate all elements synchronously.

────────────────────────

8. PHYSICAL MATERIALS

Use restrained, realistic materials.

Prefer:

aged concrete,
rough plaster,
stone,
matte painted walls,
slightly damp surfaces,
weathered masonry,
subtle dirt accumulation,
fine surface irregularities.

roughness should generally be high.

Avoid perfectly clean CG surfaces.

Avoid exaggerated texture maps.

Surface variation should mainly become visible
when grazing light passes across the material.

────────────────────────

9. ATMOSPHERE

Add only extremely subtle atmospheric depth.

Possible elements:

very light haze,
humidity,
a few dust particles,
fine rain mist,
or subtle suspended moisture.

Particles should only become visible where light reaches them.

Never create a visible particle effect across the entire scene.

────────────────────────

10. COLOR

Use a restrained low-saturation palette.

Let natural light determine most of the color.

Possible tonal families:

cool gray,
warm concrete,
muted green,
faded blue,
pale gold,
soft ivory,
deep charcoal.

Avoid teal-orange cinematic grading,
strong saturation,
fantasy blue,
neon colors,
and exaggerated bloom.

────────────────────────

11. CAMERA

Use the visual language of quiet architectural photography.

Wide but not excessively distorted lens.

Natural human eye height,
or slightly lower when appropriate.

Allow walls, ceilings, or foreground architecture
to occupy large portions of the image.

The camera does not need to face the subject directly.

Prefer observational framing,
as if the viewer quietly discovered the scene.

Avoid heroic composition.

Avoid symmetrical exhibition-style framing unless required by the space.

────────────────────────

12. PERCEPTUAL REALISM

Prioritize perceptual realism over visual spectacle.

Lighting,
material response,
scale,
shadow,
weather,
plant behavior,
and animal motion
must appear physically plausible.

The image should initially look like a real photograph.

Only after several seconds should the viewer notice
that something is slightly impossible.

────────────────────────

13. MOOD

quiet
solitary
contemplative
humid or airy
restrained
slightly uncanny
poetic
everyday
slow
impermanent

Japanese quiet architecture,
wabi-sabi,
but without obvious Japanese decorative symbols.

────────────────────────

14. STRICTLY AVOID

no clutter
no decorative furniture
no excessive plants
no fantasy architecture
no glowing magic
no neon lighting
no colorful particles
no cyberpunk
no aquarium appearance
no museum installation appearance
no luxury interior
no dramatic cinematic props
no excessive fog
no excessive bloom
no perfectly symmetrical CG composition
no obvious game environment
no exaggerated depth of field
no over-designed scenery

────────────────────────

FINAL IMAGE TARGET

A believable everyday architectural space,
mostly empty and mostly dark,
defined by one strong natural light event,
one quiet protagonist,
one subtle natural motion system,
and at most one impossible phenomenon.

The scene should feel like:

“something unusual is quietly happening here,
but the place itself does not seem to care.”
